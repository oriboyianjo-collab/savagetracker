EXHIBIT 99.1

Woodward Reports Record Sales & Earnings for Fiscal Year 2026

FORT COLLINS, Colo., Nov. 18, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- Woodward, Inc. (NASDAQ:WWD) today reported financial results for its fiscal year 2026 and fourth quarter ended September 30, 2026.

All amounts are presented on an as-reported (U.S. GAAP) basis unless otherwise indicated. All per share amounts are presented on a fully diluted basis. All references to years are references to the Company’s fiscal year unless otherwise stated. All comparisons are made to the same period of the prior year unless otherwise stated.

“Woodward delivered another year of record sales and earnings, with 22% sales growth and significant margin expansion across both segments, reflecting strong demand and disciplined execution," said Chip Blankenship, Chairman and Chief Executive Officer. "In Aerospace, higher aircraft production rates and continued strong inputs to repair centers drove double-digit growth in Commercial OEM and Services. In Industrial, strong demand across our end markets, combined with disciplined operational execution, drove significant earnings growth and margin expansion.

“We are building on this momentum by expanding manufacturing capacity, strengthening our services capabilities, and investing in technologies that provide value to our customers. These investments, together with continued operational improvements, position Woodward for solid growth in 2027. We expect to reach new levels of financial performance in 2028 and beyond as we realize the benefits of increased scale, an optimized footprint, and operational efficiencies.”

Woodward’s key highlights include:

  • Acquired Valve Research & Manufacturing, adding key technologies and capabilities to provide new Aerospace growth opportunities in commercial and defense
  • Signed Elite Licensed Repair Service Facility agreements with Lufthansa Technik, Air France Industries KLM Engineering and Maintenance, and HAECO, expanding global LEAP MRO capacity to prepare for future growth while optimizing capital
  • Signed a multi-year commercial distribution agreement naming AAR Corp. as the preferred distributor of Woodward's high-demand consumable parts for the CFM LEAP, GEnx, and CF34 engines, extending the companies' defense relationship into commercial markets
  • Expanded our Glatten, Germany, facility, increasing production capacity by 50 percent for high-speed fuel injection systems used to power data centers, broader power generation, marine transportation, and oil and gas applications
  • Continued to focus on footprint optimization to enable future growth, including construction of the Spartanburg campus to support the Airbus A350 spoiler actuation win and other product line moves and transfers between Woodward locations
  • Launched an expansion of our MRO facility in Prestwick, Scotland, that will double its capacity to service growing LEAP and GTF repair volumes
  • Selected by Airbus to supply a complete wing actuation system for its Folding Wing demonstrator, extending Woodward’s hydraulic and electromechanical actuation expertise to next-generation aircraft wing design
  • Won Gold for Manufacturing Employer of the Year at the 2026 Stevie Awards for Great Employers
  • Named to Fast Company’s World’s Most Innovative Companies of 2026
  • Named to the 2026 Fortune 100 Fastest-Growing Companies list

About Woodward, Inc.
Woodward is the global leader in the design, manufacture, and service of energy conversion and control solutions for the aerospace and industrial equipment markets. Our purpose is to design and deliver energy control solutions our partners count on to power a clean future. Our innovative fluid, combustion, electrical, propulsion, and motion control systems perform in some of the world’s harshest environments. Woodward is a global company headquartered in Fort Collins, Colorado, USA. Visit our website at www.woodward.com.

1Adjusted and Non-U.S. GAAP Financial Measures: Adjusted net earnings, adjusted earnings per share, adjusted income tax expense, adjusted effective tax rate, adjusted EBIT, adjusted EBITDA, and adjusted nonsegment expenses exclude, as applicable, (i) product rationalization, (ii) costs related to business development activities, (iii) restructuring charges, (iv) a specific charge for excess and obsolete inventory, and (v) the impact of a German corporate tax rate reduction. The product rationalization adjustment pertains to the elimination and divestiture of certain product lines. The specific charge for excess and obsolete inventory relates to the intended disposal of certain inventory in our Industrial segment due in part to an unexpected shift in sales patterns. This non-recurring charge is not related to product rationalization and reflects an isolated event. The Company believes that these excluded items are short‐term in nature, not directly related to the ongoing operations of the business, and therefore, the exclusion of them illustrates more clearly how the underlying business of Woodward is performing.

EBIT (earnings before interest and taxes), adjusted EBIT, EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization), adjusted EBITDA, adjusted net earnings, adjusted earnings per share, adjusted income tax expense, adjusted effective tax rate, adjusted nonsegment expenses, EBITDA leverage, and free cash flow are financial measures not prepared and presented in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of America (U.S. GAAP). Management uses EBIT and adjusted EBIT to evaluate Woodward’s operating performance without the impacts of financing and tax-related considerations. Management uses EBITDA and adjusted EBITDA in evaluating Woodward’s operating performance, making business decisions, including developing budgets, managing expenditures, forecasting future periods, and evaluating capital structure impacts of various strategic scenarios. EBITDA leverage is calculated by taking total debt divided by rolling twelve-month EBITDA. Management uses EBITDA leverage to assess Woodward’s earnings capacity relative to its total debt, monitor financial flexibility, evaluate capital structure impacts of strategic scenarios, and assist in capital allocation decisions. Management also uses free cash flow, which is derived from net cash provided by or used in operating activities less payments for property, plant, and equipment, in reviewing the financial performance of Woodward’s business segments and evaluating cash generation levels. Securities analysts, investors, and others frequently use EBIT, EBITDA, and free cash flow in their evaluation of companies, particularly those with significant property, plant, and equipment, and intangible assets that are subject to amortization. The use of any of these non-U.S. GAAP financial measures is not intended to be considered in isolation of, or as a substitute for, the financial information prepared and presented in accordance with U.S. GAAP. Because adjusted net earnings, adjusted earnings per share, EBIT, EBITDA, adjusted EBIT, adjusted EBITDA, and EBITDA leverage exclude certain financial information compared with net earnings, the most comparable U.S. GAAP financial measure, users of this financial information should consider the information that is excluded. Free cash flow does not necessarily represent funds available for discretionary use and is not necessarily a measure of our ability to fund our cash needs. Management’s calculations of EBIT, EBITDA, adjusted net earnings, adjusted earnings per share, adjusted EBIT, adjusted EBITDA, adjusted effective tax rate, adjusted nonsegment expenses, EBITDA leverage, and free cash flow may differ from similarly titled measures used by other companies, limiting their usefulness as comparative measures.
