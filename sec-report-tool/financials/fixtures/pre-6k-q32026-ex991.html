1 Annualized Revenue Run-Rate ("ARR") is an operating metric calculated by multiplying IM8 revenue recognized in the applicable month by twelve and includes revenue from both subscription and one-time purchases. ARR is not a financial measure under IFRS, and is not necessarily indicative of revenue for any future period. ARR reflects orders from both new and existing customers and incorporates the effect of cancellations and non-renewals occurring on or before the applicable month; however, it does not adjust for anticipated future cancellations, non-renewals or pauses. ARR annualizes a single month of revenue and assumes that the revenue recognized in that month is maintained for the following twelve months. As a result, ARR is sensitive to the timing of promotional activity, product launches and billing cycles. In particular, the full order value of quarterly subscription plans is recognized upon shipment of the three-month supply, which increases revenue recognized in months when quarterly billings are concentrated. It may therefore differ significantly from actual future revenue.

New customers include subscription and one-time orders across all channels, products and plan types.

NEW YORK, N.Y. – November 18, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) – Prenetics Global Limited (Nasdaq: PRE) (“Prenetics” or the “Company”), a leading consumer health company and parent of the AI-native direct-to-consumer wellness brand IM8, co-founded by Sir David Beckham, today reported its third quarter 2026 financial results. The complete results are presented in Prenetics’ Q3 2026 Letter to Shareholders (the “Shareholder Letter”), which includes all financial tables, together with an accompanying Q3 2026 Investor Presentation (the “Investor Presentation”), each dated November 18, 2026 and available on the Company’s Investor Relations website at https://ir.prenetics.com.
Danny Yeung, Co-Founder and CEO of Prenetics and IM8, said: “This was a record quarter, with revenue above the top of our guidance, and our case for investing in IM8 has never been clearer. So we are investing more. The 27,000 customers we acquired in our first full quarter have already returned about 4.1 times their acquisition cost in gross profit, in under two years, buying only our core products. That is gross profit already collected, not a forecast, and three to one is the benchmark subscription businesses are widely measured against. The next 91,000 customers are on pace to pass it in early 2027. Customers we acquire at today’s cost are earning it back in about six months, and independent card data shows our customers staying longer than those of the three leading brands we are compared with, as we detail in our Shareholder Letter and Investor Presentation. Returns like these are why we now expect a second-half Adjusted EBITDA loss of $30 to $35 million. That is our choice. We will keep investing at this pace for as long as the returns hold, and not a day longer.”
“We are making that choice from strength. We ended the quarter with $111.5 million of cash and current financial assets, more than we started with, and a $1 billion commitment from General Catalyst behind our marketing. That frees our own capital for what keeps customers with us: new products, three clinical trials now underway, and inventory built ahead of demand. The business we have already built earned $30.6 million of Contribution Profit this quarter, before acquisition marketing and brand partner costs. If we stopped acquiring new customers tomorrow, our existing subscribers alone would make this company profitable on an Adjusted EBITDA basis. We keep going because every customer we bring in today becomes more valuable with each product and channel we add. In under two years we have taken IM8 from nothing to an Annualized Revenue Run-Rate of about $284 million, which we believe no brand in this category has done before. 2027 will be bigger still: a core business we expect to exceed $400 million of revenue, and on top of it IM8 Hydration in the first quarter, Care Bears by IM8 in the second quarter, and our first retail shelves and China by the end of the year, none of which is in that number. This is what I have lived for.”


Brian Rosin, Chief Financial Officer Designate of Prenetics, said: “Six months into my role at IM8, what stands out is how well the numbers hold up when you take them apart, cohort by cohort. It is rare to see growth at this rate with returns that can be measured this precisely. This quarter, revenue came in above the top of the range we gave in August, Customer Acquisition Cost declined for the second consecutive quarter while we spent more, and Adjusted EBITDA improved to a loss of $12.7 million, or 20% of revenue, from 39% in the second quarter. Net cash used in operating activities was $27.5 million, and with $31.6 million of net fundings under our General Catalyst facility, Adjusted Free Cash Flow was positive $4.1 million. Our cash and current financial assets grew during a quarter of record investment. We now expect a second-half Adjusted EBITDA loss of $30 to $35 million, compared with the $8 to $12 million we guided to in August. At the midpoint of our guidance ranges, that is about 23% of second-half revenue, compared with about 30% in the first half. It is a deliberate increase in customer acquisition investment, 70% of which is financed by our General Catalyst facility. On everything I have seen in six months here, the case for making that investment is strong, and I am excited about what is ahead.”

UNAUDITED NON-IFRS FINANCIAL MEASURES
To supplement the Company’s consolidated financial statements prepared in accordance with International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS), the Company is providing the following non-IFRS measures: EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA, Contribution Profit, Contribution Margin and Adjusted Free Cash Flow. These non-IFRS financial and liquidity measures are not based on any standardized methodology prescribed by IFRS and are not necessarily comparable to similarly-titled measures presented by other companies. Management believes these non-IFRS financial and liquidity measures are useful to investors in evaluating the Company’s ongoing operating results and trends and in facilitating period-to-period comparisons of the Company’s performance.
EBITDA is defined as loss for the period under IFRS Accounting Standards before (1) depreciation and amortization, (2) interest income, (3) other finance costs, and (4) income tax expense.
Adjusted EBITDA is defined as EBITDA further adjusted to exclude (1) amortization of deferred expenses, (2) equity-settled share-based payment expenses, (3) transaction-related expenses associated with acquisition, disposal and fundraising activities, (4) strategic realignment and discontinued products impact, (5) exchange gain or loss, net, (6) fair value (gain)/loss on financial assets at fair value through profit or loss, (7) fair value (gain)/loss on warrant liabilities, (8) unrealized fair value loss on digital assets, (9) realized fair value gain on digital assets, and (10) (profit)/loss from discontinued operations, net of tax.
Contribution Profit is defined as Adjusted EBITDA further adjusted to exclude acquisition marketing expenses, brand royalty, and ambassador contracts and licensing fees.
Contribution Margin, a non-IFRS financial measure, is calculated as Contribution Profit divided by revenue. Management believes Contribution Profit and Contribution Margin are useful to investors because they present the profitability of the business before growth investment. Growth investment comprises (1) customer-acquisition marketing, which is discretionary in timing and amount and which management evaluates as a capital-allocation decision against expected cohort returns, funded in part through the CVF facility; and (2) brand royalty and ambassador contract and licensing costs, which, while contractually committed, represent investments in long-term brand equity entered into to support that growth strategy rather than costs of serving the existing customer base. These measures have limitations: growth investment has been and will continue to be a significant recurring cash expense, brand royalty varies with revenue, and Contribution Profit should not be viewed as profitability achievable absent such spending.
Adjusted Free Cash Flow, a non-IFRS liquidity measure, is defined as net cash (used in) from operating activities determined in accordance with IFRS Accounting Standards, less capital expenditures, plus net fundings under the General Catalyst Customer Value Fund (CVF) facility.


This non-IFRS liquidity measure depends on the continued availability of the CVF facility funding, and is not a measure of cash generated from operations, and does not represent residual cash available for discretionary use. “Net fundings” represents, for the relevant period, funding amounts received under the CVF facility (“Investment Amounts,” as defined in the Customer Investment Agreement dated July 13, 2026) less repayments of Investment Amounts and interest paid under the facility.
In addition, other companies, including companies in the same industry, may not use the same non-IFRS measures or may calculate these metrics in a different manner than management, or may use other financial measures to evaluate their performance, all of which could reduce the usefulness of these non-IFRS measures as comparative measures. Because of these limitations, the Company’s non-IFRS financial measures should not be considered in isolation from, or as a substitute for, financial information prepared in accordance with IFRS Accounting Standards.
The Company has not reconciled its outlook for Adjusted EBITDA to loss for the period or Adjusted Free Cash Flow to net cash (used in) from operating activities, their most directly comparable measures under IFRS Accounting Standards. Items such as fair value changes on warrant liabilities and financial assets, share-based payment expenses, and the timing of funding and repayments under the General Catalyst facility cannot be predicted with reasonable certainty. Accordingly, these reconciliations are not available without unreasonable effort. These items could materially affect the corresponding IFRS results.
